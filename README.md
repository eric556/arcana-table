# Arcana — a manual Magic tabletop

A browser-based tabletop for up to four friends, with Commander (40 life) and 60-card (20 life) starting settings. Game rules, deck legality, mana, combat, card effects, wins and losses are resolved by the players.

## Play

1. Open the app. You start in a fresh, empty room with one real seat and no sample cards or opponents. You can import your deck before hosting; opening the room keeps your cards. Leaving a room returns to an empty room, and a failed connection keeps your prepared cards.
2. Choose **Host table**, enter a name and format, and keep the host tab open.
3. Share the invite with friends who have access to the app. They choose **Join table** or open the invite and enter their name.
4. Each person imports their own deck. In Moxfield, export a text list (MTGO format) and paste it into **Deck list**. Commander headings and `*CMDR*` annotations work. Sideboards remain private and separate. You can also open a .txt deck list. URL imports are not supported.
5. Draw an opening seven. Drag cards into zones, or select a card and choose a destination in the inspector. Double-click a nonland card to cast it to the shared stack, or double-click a land to play it directly. Double-click a permanent to tap it. Draw, mill, shuffle, mulligan, untap, tokens, counters, life, poison, commander damage and phase/turn controls are manual.

The field is a shared table with a playmat for each player. Each mat fits three rows of six cards. Mats sit across a narrow central gap, with the opposite seats rotated to face you. Additional cards can be placed in the large exterior territory around each mat. Whole table frames the mats and cards in play rather than the unused territory; Your area shows the extra space. The floating hand stays above all battlefield cards. With a mouse it tucks down until hovered (keyboard focus also raises it); touch screens keep it exposed. Dropping a permanent over its own card repositions it on the underlying battlefield instead of targeting or attaching to itself. The hand has no bottom toolbar: use Cards beside your life total for hand inspection, drawing, shuffling, mulligans, and importing. Right-click the command pile (or tap its menu button) to cast a commander and manually increase/decrease commander tax by 2. Tax belongs to each individual card and survives zone moves and room recovery; casting does not automatically increase it. Drag empty space to pan, scroll/pinch to zoom, or use the player, Whole table, and Stack shortcuts. Cards retain free placement on their battlefield. Click public graveyard/exile/command piles to inspect them; opponent libraries open approval requests. Face-down hands show counts only. Drag an opponent's battlefield card or use its menu to request movement permission. Approval does not change control or reveal hidden information.

**Shared stack** is one host-ordered list of spells and abilities for the whole room, with the newest response on top. Casting from the hand, command zone, graveyard, or exile moves that card to the stack; dragging a nonland from hand onto your battlefield also casts it. Direct manual zone moves remain available. **Add ability** creates a named entry with optional source, effect, and targets without moving its source. Everyone sees stack additions and can respond, signal **No response**, manually resolve the top entry to a chosen zone, or counter an entry. Ready signals reset after changes and never automatically resolve anything. Abilities are removed on resolution; players apply all effects and costs themselves. Spell resolution and card movement are a single host transaction. Stack revisions reject stale resolution requests, and duplicate clicks cannot move a card twice. A disconnected original owner does not block resolution.

Use **Top X cards** in the library context menu to privately look at or publicly reveal the top N cards. Each viewed card has a destination selector for hand, graveyard, exile, battlefield, command zone, stack, sideboard, library top, or library bottom. Drag rows or use the arrow buttons to choose the order, then apply the entire batch. The top list is ordered next-to-draw first. The bottom list runs closest-to-top first, with its last card becoming the bottommost. Pending choices do not modify the deck. Closing the panel leaves its order unchanged and stops the reveal. A shared Revealed cards strip lets everyone inspect cards their owners chose to reveal.

**Cards → View hand**, **Browse battlefield**, and the regular zone panels also support selecting several cards and moving them in a chosen order. This supports returning multiple cards from any owned zone to the top or bottom. All moves in a batch are validated before any are applied; duplicate or stale selections cannot cause a partial move.

During **Combat**, drag a battlefield card onto another player’s board to assign it as an attacker. Defenders drag their own cards onto an attacker targeting them, either on its battlefield or in the combat lane. Multiple blockers can pair with one attacker. Attackers and blockers appear as physical card pairings on the table. Drag a paired card back to your battlefield (or your player bar) to undo it; dragging an attacker to a different player retargets it. Ownership, targets, and stale-combat IDs are validated by the host. Cards remain in their actual battlefield zones, with no automatic tapping, damage, or keyword enforcement. Cards leaving play and phase changes clear obsolete pairings.

The **stack is always visible beside the battlefield**, as a pile of card images. Drag a spell onto it to cast or respond; drag a permanent onto it to create an illustrated ability entry while keeping its source in play. **Resolve top card** is prominently displayed beside the cards, with its destination shown. Alternatively drag the top card onto Resolve or its owner’s battlefield zone, graveyard, exile, command zone, library, or hand. Dragging a lower spell to its owner’s graveyard or exile marks it countered. **Spread cards** exposes each image. Advanced stack actions and custom ability text are still available as secondary controls. Mouse dragging, touch dragging with a card ghost, and Space-to-pick-up / select-destination keyboard input all use the same gesture handler. The central battlefield scrolls separately, keeping the stack and hand within reach.

Keyboard shortcuts: D draws one, U untaps all, S shuffles, ? opens help. A mulligan returns the hand to the library and draws seven; players bottom cards themselves.

## Network and privacy model

PeerJS uses WebRTC data channels. A browser hosts the roster, public board and turn marker; PeerJS Cloud provides signaling. No cloud game database is used. Each player keeps their full private state locally and sends only a redacted public projection to the host. The host never receives guest hands, unrevealed library identities/order, or sideboards. Explicitly revealed library cards are shared with everyone until hidden, moved, shuffled, or the viewing session ends. Face-down public cards have their metadata removed. A private top-X look shares only the fact that a player looked at N cards; the viewed IDs and identities stay local. Drawing a revealed card never reveals an additional card automatically. Incoming guest updates are bound to the seat assigned to that connection. This is a trusted, casual tabletop, not a competitive anti-cheat system: players control and could falsify their own state.

Many home networks connect directly. Some NAT/firewall combinations require a TURN server; URL, username and credential can be entered under Connection settings. Relay credentials stay in memory for the current tab. Play pauses while the host is offline. The host can refresh and resume the saved room from its checkpoint; guests reconnect to their reserved seats and receive a complete filtered snapshot. Pending commands are retried exactly once. Host recovery is stored in localStorage and guest seats/outboxes in sessionStorage. Clearing those stores removes recovery; leaving deliberately clears the saved room or seat. Four seats includes the host.

The app's hosting access policy is independent of room codes: a friend must be allowed to open the site as well as have its room invitation.

## Scryfall and caching

Deck resolution batches up to 75 identifiers per collection request, waits at least 650 ms between request starts, deduplicates names, and caches card records locally. A Web Lock coordinates lookup traffic across same-origin tabs where available. A 429 stops the import and applies at least a 60-second cooldown, honoring longer numeric Retry-After values. There are no automatic rate-limit retries. Browser fetch supplies the browser's User-Agent and the client sends Accept headers.

Art downloads use a separate serialized queue with a 120 ms pause. Cache Storage holds downloaded art and metadata on that device; already cached art is not fetched again. Twenty-eight starter card images are bundled with the app and use normal static-file caching. Browsers can evict local storage; persistence is best effort. Cards and artwork are supplied by Scryfall; Magic and artwork belong to Wizards of the Coast and the respective artists. See https://scryfall.com/docs/api/rate-limits and https://scryfall.com/docs/api/cards/collection.

## Development and verification

Install with the existing pnpm lockfile. `pnpm dev` starts the app in a normal development setup; the managed Sites environment uses its supervised preview command. `pnpm build` builds the Cloudflare-compatible server and client. `node tests/core.mjs` verifies gameplay invariants, Moxfield parsing, public-state privacy, seat ownership, capacity, turn authorization, per-defender combat declarations, stale-combat rejection, and reconnect behavior using an in-memory transport. `node tests/gestures.mjs` checks physical gesture interpretation, attack/block ownership and cleanup, four-seat synchronization, private-card isolation, and rendered combat/stack affordances. `node tests/stack.mjs` verifies shared ordering, responses, manual resolution, concurrency, acknowledgements, abilities, and private-card isolation. `pnpm exec tsc --noEmit` checks TypeScript.

Core tests do not establish that external signaling or WebRTC will work across any particular two networks. Live cross-network play should be tried with the intended host and a friend, with TURN configured if required. Deck import uses pasted text or .txt files; it does not contact Moxfield.


Targeting and attachments: Drop a spell from your hand onto a player or battlefield card to cast it and mark that target. Drag a stack card onto additional targets; repeating a target removes it. Targets are shared with all seats and highlighted on the battlefield. Dropping onto a target never transfers ownership or resolves the card. Resolve remains an explicit action. Your battlefield remains on the same table while viewing other seats.

Auras resolved to the battlefield tuck beneath their first target. Drag an aura, equipment, or fortification already on your battlefield onto a permanent to attach it; drag it onto Your battlefield to detach. Attachments remain owned by their original player and appear beneath the target even across players. When a target leaves play, its attachments return to their owner's normal battlefield display; graveyard moves, effects, and legality remain manual. During your combat, dropping a permanent onto another player still declares an attack. Pan between playmats to inspect permanents.

Ability suggestions use cached Scryfall Oracle text and preserve the original paragraph, including modal bullets. Common activated, loyalty, equip, and triggered text is recognized. This is a text-selection aid, not a full rules parser; unusual text has a custom ability fallback. Static effects and costs are never executed. New card records preserve per-face text and keywords; existing cached text continues to work without refetching artwork.

The host owns canonical state and accepts validated commands from each seat. Reconnection replaces local state with a current filtered snapshot. Protocol 9 requires everyone to refresh and start a new room. See [exile and sync](docs/exile-and-sync.md) for recovery, privacy, movement approvals, and borrowed exile piles.


Joining after an update: room-format compatibility is separate from normal app updates. Format 4 remains unchanged for connection-error fixes. A mismatch now says whether the host or joining tab needs refreshing; old hosts that do not report a format get instructions to refresh both, create a new room, and use the new invite. Refreshing the host ends the active room. The original error remains visible instead of being overwritten by a disconnect message. Connection settings shows the loaded room format. The root app document is served with `Cache-Control: no-store`; card artwork keeps its existing local cache. `node tests/versions.mjs` exercises current, older, and newer room handshakes and legacy errors.


Resolution recovery: owners resend their public acknowledgement whenever a pending snapshot arrives, including when their local card already moved. This repairs a lost acknowledgement without moving the card twice. The host retries pending snapshots after 2, 4, and 8 seconds. The stack names the owner/card/destination it is waiting on and offers Retry resolution for a fresh snapshot; it never forces a move while an owner is offline.

Guest refresh recovery: the current player, private zones, exact library order, room code, seat token, format, and connection settings are saved in this tab’s session storage. Ordinary refresh restores the player immediately and shows Reconnect saved seat. The header offers Reconnect during play; Connection settings also offers Refresh & reconnect, which saves first and reconnects once after reload. Saving failures stop that controlled refresh and show an error. Explicit reconnect may replace the seat’s stale previous connection; an ordinary duplicate-tab join cannot evict a live seat. Existing saved seats from the previous release remain readable. The host must remain open; host reload/room restoration is not implemented. Room format stays 4. `node tests/recovery.mjs` drops actual simulated peer messages, refreshes an owner during resolution, and reclaims a seat in a full four-player room while checking card conservation and private-zone isolation.

Token stacks: custom tokens accept blank power/toughness and a quantity from 1 to 9,999. One card instance represents that count (`tokenCount`, default 1); tapping, counters and moves affect the whole stack. Tokens sent to graveyard or exile cease to exist before the updated state is published, including whole stacks, batch moves and approved opponent exile. Right-click a battlefield token for Copy token or Copy / split tokens; these controls also appear in its inspector. Copies form a new untapped stack without counters. Splits preserve state and attack assignment. The host atomically splits one attacker and one blocker when a blocking gesture involves a stack; remaining tokens stay grouped. Drop subsequent blockers on the remainder to block more tokens, or on the separated attacker to multi-block. The Combat camera button opens paired cards plus available attackers/blockers. Damage and effects remain manual. Token commands and split identities are checkpointed with the authoritative room and sequence receipts; replay cannot duplicate a split. `node tests/tokens.mjs` covers counts, control, combat splitting, recovery, and pile casting.

Pile dragging: the visible top card of command, graveyard and exile can be dragged directly to the stack or a destination. Dropping a spell on your battlefield casts it via the stack; lands enter the battlefield. Approved borrowed cards retain their owner and play restrictions. Your library's top card can also be dragged, with a face-down preview; an opponent's private library never becomes draggable. Clicking a public pile still browses it, and clicking your library still draws.

Deck import transport: PeerJS uses reliable binary serialization for automatic chunking of JSON message strings (including join payloads, decks and snapshots). Its JSON serializer rejects messages at 16,300 bytes, which previously stranded imports at “Deck ready.” UI command waits now expire after 20 seconds or reject on disconnect/send errors; the guest's journal remains intact for exactly-once retry. A pending deck cannot be replaced by a duplicate import. The importer names the host-confirmation step and offers Reconnect to host after a failure. Room format and existing checkpoints remain unchanged. Recovery tests exercise the installed PeerJS encoders/reassembly with a 100-card deck, lost replies, host storage errors, timeouts, and disconnects; the RTC/signaling layer is simulated.

Leaving and resetting: “New game” / “Leave” in the header, “Start fresh” beside saved-room recovery, and the cancel/reset actions in join/import dialogs all use the same reset. It closes this tab’s connection, forgets its saved seat and pending commands, clears automatic-rejoin flags and the invitation fragment, and starts an empty game. The matching hosted checkpoint is removed when ending a hosted room; unrelated host tabs and card caches are preserved. A per-tab fresh-start marker prevents another saved hosted room from immediately replacing the new game on refresh. Explicitly joining/hosting again enables normal recovery. Canceled async imports and connection attempts cannot restore old room state. `node tests/room-reset.mjs` checks recovery cleanup.

Changing control: drag another player’s battlefield permanent onto your own battlefield, or choose “Ask to take control” in its card menu. The current controller approves; no card moves before approval. The host retains the original owner while moving the permanent’s presentation/control to the requester, preserves counters/tapped/face state and attachments, and clears combat assignments for that permanent. It behaves like your other permanents for tapping, counters, abilities and combat; moves to hand, library, graveyard or exile still go to its owner’s zones. “Return control to owner,” or dragging it back to the owner’s battlefield, returns it without another approval. Stale approvals are rejected if the permanent moved or changed controllers. Original owners cannot edit a battlefield permanent controlled by someone else; they can request it back. Authority tests cover approval, control chains, ownership, attachments, face-down privacy and reconnect snapshots.

Combat is declared on the shared table: drag onto an opponent’s player header, camera shortcut, battlefield, or planeswalker. Cards retain their positions; orange arrows show attacks and blue dashed arrows show blocks. The attacker chooses **Done declaring attackers** before defenders can block. **Edit attackers** clears blocks and invalidates stale combat gestures. Planeswalker control or zone changes prune obsolete attacks. Damage and tapping remain manual.

### Compact table and card handling
- Room/connection actions live in the Table menu; phase and pass-turn controls stay in a compact header. Camera navigation sits underneath (84px total before contextual combat controls).
- Hover or keyboard-focus a card: T tap/untap, G graveyard, E exile, H hand, C cast/play current face, B battlefield, F flip, V inspect. D/U/S and ? retain draw/untap all/shuffle/help. Typing, menus, dialogs, composition, modifiers and repeated keydown suppress shortcuts. Permissions still apply; shortcuts never bypass shared stack resolution.
- Rooms display as landscape permanents with upright badges and normal tap behavior. Layer → front/forward/backward/back adjusts synchronized visual ordering without changing placement; the host validates controller or movement permission, including borrowed cards.
- Hand / Exile switches the floating fan. Approved borrowed exile cards are grouped with the permitted player's exile fan while retaining original ownership and the exile zone. Granted private art is hydrated only for the authorized viewer; public fans retain face-down backs. Expiry/revocation and game effects remain manual.
- Regression checks: tests/card-shortcuts.mjs, tests/authority.mjs, tests/shared-cards.mjs, tests/positioning.mjs, tests/rooms-adventures.mjs.

- Offer control to… lets the current controller choose another connected player. The recipient accepts or declines; the sender can cancel. Pending offers survive reconnect, stale offers fail, and original ownership stays unchanged. Death or renewed exile sends borrowed permanents to the original owner’s zone.

Tidy arranges controlled permanents into three rows: creatures first (including mixed types), other permanents second, lands third. Large creature groups spill into row two; crowded rows overlap. Attachments and card state are preserved. Hand reveals, library reveals and face-up exile reveals share a floating card popup with manual hide/minimize. Only selected hand identities enter public snapshots. A separate Playable exile hand sits beside the private hand on each mat; ordinary exile remains separate. Dragging foreign public cards there creates an owner-approved exile request. Cards keep original-owner custody, play expiry remains manual, and the permitted player can return cards to ordinary exile. Regression coverage: tests/tabletop-features.mjs.

Hand interaction: hover animates only artwork; fixed, non-overlapping grab areas prevent cards from jumping between pointer targets. Large hands keep readable strips and scroll with wheel, trackpad, horizontal touch swipe, or small arrow controls. Keyboard focus scrolls the complete card into view. Native drag retains a full-card ghost.

Tidy groups lands by basic land types, falling back to produced mana colors, and places duplicate names together with tighter overlap. Creature precedence and the three-row arrangement remain unchanged. Ability/oracle text renders brace tokens using bundled Scryfall SVG symbols; unknown tokens retain their text.

Stack target arrows: the floating stack traces the top entry by default; hovering or focusing another stack card traces only that entry. Multiple visible targets are numbered. Lines follow each viewer’s camera and disappear when the entry resolves or the floating stack closes. Offscreen targets retain their textual label; no hidden card information is added to snapshots.

Defender readiness: after attackers are confirmed, each attacked player (including planeswalker controllers) chooses I am done declaring, even with no blocks. Shared ready counts and waiting names appear in Combat controls. The final confirmation triggers a table-wide toast and a persistent ready-to-resolve message; responses and damage remain manual. Edit my blockers or a changed block clears that defender’s confirmation; removed attackers/blockers invalidate affected defenders, and reopening attackers clears all confirmations. Offline defenders remain required and ready state survives host checkpoints and reconnect snapshots. Coverage: tests/combat-ready.mjs.

Tapped-card labels: counters, token quantities and combat badges live in an overlay matching the rotated card’s visible footprint. Labels remain upright, including opposite seats. Empty layout wrappers no longer intercept clicks beside tapped cards. Battlefield context menus offer Add / Remove +1/+1 counter and Manage counters; existing inspector controls accept named counters without untapping.
